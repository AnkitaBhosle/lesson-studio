# Rubric (draft for the owner to edit)

The judge model scores every output against this, 1–5 per criterion. The owner spot-checks about 20% blind. If owner and judge agree less than ~80% of the time (within 1 point), fix the rubric before trusting results.

Anchors: write one sentence for 1, 3 and 5; 2 and 4 sit between.

## All planning outputs
| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Teaching structure** | A list of facts; no hook, no arc, no recap | A sensible order, but the hook or the ending is weak | Hook → build-up → example → common mistake → recap; each slide earns its place |
| **Audience fit** | Wrong level: jargon for beginners, or basics for experts | Mostly right level; a few terms undefined | Every term defined on first use; examples feel made for this audience |
| **Accuracy and grounding** | A wrong fact, or a cited claim the source doesn't support | Facts right, but some claims lack citations or flags | Every factual claim cites a source or is flagged for the teacher |
| **Plain language** | Production or AI jargon reaches the teacher (models, credits, "scene") | Clear but wordy | Reads like a colleague's lesson outline |
| **Follows guidelines** | Breaks a "must" guideline | Follows them, but one is only partly met | Every guideline met and visibly checked |

## Task-specific checks (pass / fail)
- **Revision:** did what the note asked; slides it didn't need to touch are unchanged; changed slides are marked.
- **Story angles:** the three angles are genuinely different ways to teach it, not rewordings.
- **Recap suggestion:** only suggested when an earlier lesson really sets this one up.
- **Self-check:** found the planted problem; no false alarms on clean slides.
- **Preference learning:** learned the intended preference; learned nothing from one-off edits; never relearned a removed preference.

## Source extraction
| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Faithfulness** | States something the source doesn't say | All true, a few paraphrases drift | Every fact traceable to its locator |
| **Coverage** | Misses most must-extract facts | Gets most | Gets all, plus the right key concepts |
| **Gap detection** | Misses the gap, or invents one | Finds it, vaguely | Names the gap in plain words and suggests what would fill it |
