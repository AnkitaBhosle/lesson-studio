## Source
ok so this is for Tuesday, knife safety, the first lab, um, before they touch anything.

so first thing, the claw. Claw grip. Fingertips tucked under like a cat paw, knuckles out front, and the flat of the blade kind of rides on your knuckles. That way your fingertips are never under the blade. I want to demo that first and then walk around and check every single person, because they'll say they've got it and they don't.

sharp knives. People think a sharp knife is the dangerous one, it's the opposite, a dull knife slips, you push harder, that's when it goes into your hand. so sharp is safer. we'll do honing and sharpening next week, not this week, don't get into it.

oh and the boards, damp towel under the board, or the rubber mats, whatever's in the bin, so the board doesn't slide around. That one's easy to forget.

if a knife falls, you do not catch it. step back. let it fall. I'll probably say that twice.

walking with a knife, point down, blade down by your side, and you say "knife behind" or "sharp behind" when you go behind somebody. Same as on the line.

um, the sink. Never put a knife in the sink with soapy water, you can't see it and the dishwasher, the person washing, reaches in. Wash it right away, dry it, put it back on the magnet.

and the claw again, yeah, also when they're doing the onions, the claw, because the onion rolls.

cuts. If it's small, pressure with a clean towel, hold it up, and tell me. Even if it's tiny. First aid kit's by the dish station. And the mandoline, cut glove every time, no exceptions, I don't care if you've done it at home.

ok I think that's it. claw, sharp, towel, don't catch, point down, sink. yeah.
