## Plan
1. Hook — "Eight glasses a day: where did that come from?"
2. Why water matters — Water helps prevent dehydration and keeps the body working normally. (Source: CDC, About Water and Healthier Drinks)
3. How much, according to the National Academies — About 2.7 liters a day of total water for women and 3.7 liters for men, counting all drinks and food. (Source: National Academies, 2004)
4. The official rule — Experts recommend eight 8-ounce glasses of plain water a day. (Source: National Academies, 2004)
5. Food counts too — About 20% of the water people take in comes from food. (Source: National Academies, 2004)
6. When you need more — Hot weather, being more active, fever, diarrhea or vomiting. (Source: CDC, About Water and Healthier Drinks)
7. Thirst is a good guide — Most healthy people meet their needs by drinking when thirsty and with meals. (Source: National Academies, 2004)
8. Recap — Needs vary; drink when you're thirsty and more when it's hot or you're active; talk to a doctor about your own needs.
