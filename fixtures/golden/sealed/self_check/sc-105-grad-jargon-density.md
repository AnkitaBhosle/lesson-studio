## Plan
1. Title — Sink or float? The secret is density.
2. What density means — Density is how much mass is packed into a space: mass divided by volume. (Source: USGS Water Density)
3. Water is the benchmark — Water's density is about 1 gram per cubic centimeter. Things denser than that sink; less dense things float. (Source: USGS Water Density)
4. Ice floats — Ice is less dense than liquid water, so about a tenth of an iceberg sticks out above the water. (Source: USGS Water Density)
5. Why water is weird — Density is an intensive thermodynamic property; water's ρ(T) peaks near 277 K because the tetrahedral hydrogen-bond network produces anomalous expansion below that temperature. (Source: USGS Water Density)
6. Lakes freeze from the top — The densest water sinks, so ice forms on the surface first. (Source: USGS Water Density)
7. Salt makes water denser — Seawater is denser than fresh water because of the dissolved salt. (Source: USGS Water Density)
8. Safety first — Goggles on, glass cylinders in the middle of the table, tell me about spills.
9. Lab — Measure mass and volume, calculate density, predict sink or float.
10. Check for understanding — "Why does the rock sink but the wood float?"
