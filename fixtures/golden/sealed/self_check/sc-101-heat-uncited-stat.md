## Plan
1. Title — Working safely in the heat. Photo of a crew mowing a sports field.
2. Heat illness is a range — From heat rash and cramps to heat stroke, the most serious. (Source: CDC NIOSH, Heat-related Illnesses)
3. Heat stroke can move fast — Body temperature can reach 106°F or higher within 10 to 15 minutes. (Source: CDC NIOSH, Heat stroke)
4. New to the heat? Take it slow — Most heat deaths on the job happen in a worker's first few days working in the heat.
5. Spot heat exhaustion — Headache, nausea, dizziness, weakness, heavy sweating, thirst. (Source: CDC NIOSH, Heat exhaustion)
6. Help with heat exhaustion — Move to shade, small sips of cool water, loosen and remove extra clothing, stay with them, get medical care. (Source: CDC NIOSH, Heat exhaustion first aid)
7. Heat stroke means 911 — Call 911, cool them fast with cold water or ice on the head, neck, armpits and groin. (Source: CDC NIOSH, Heat stroke first aid)
8. Recap — Know the signs, stop early, tell your supervisor.
