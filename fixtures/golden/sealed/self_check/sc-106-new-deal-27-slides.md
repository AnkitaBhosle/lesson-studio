## Plan
Section: Where things stood in 1933
1. Title — The New Deal: relief, recovery, reform. Image: FDR at a desk with microphones (alt text: President Roosevelt seated at a desk covered with radio microphones).
2. Where we left off — Bank failures, mass unemployment and Hoover's limited response. (Source: First New Deal, opening)
3. Roosevelt's plan — Relief and recovery first, then long-term reform through federal regulation. (Source: First New Deal)
4. The banking crisis — Over five thousand banks had closed; New York and Illinois had ordered closures to stop bank runs. (Source: First New Deal)
Section: The first hundred days
5. The bank holiday — Within 48 hours of the inauguration, a national bank holiday and a special session of Congress. (Source: First New Deal)
6. Emergency Banking Act — Signed March 9, 1933, eight hours after Congress first saw it. (Source: First New Deal)
7. The first fireside chat — March 12, 1933: Roosevelt explains the bank inspections on the radio and confidence returns. Image: radio set (alt text: a 1930s wooden table radio). (Source: First New Deal)
8. Primary source — Short excerpt from the first fireside chat for students to annotate. (Source: First New Deal, fireside chat excerpt)
9. Glass-Steagall and the FDIC — June 1933: commercial banks separated from investment banking; the new FDIC insures deposits up to $250,000. (Source: First New Deal)
10. Jobs now — FERA sends $500 million in direct grants to states. (Source: First New Deal)
11. Putting people to work — At its peak the CWA employed some four million people on roads, bridges and airports. (Source: First New Deal)
12. The CCC — Young men from relief families plant trees and build parks for $30 a month. Image: CCC crew planting trees (alt text: a line of young men in work clothes planting saplings on a hillside). (Source: First New Deal)
Section: Recovery for farms and factories
13. The AAA — May 12, 1933: farmers paid to cut production so prices rise. (Source: First New Deal)
14. Plowing under — A 1933 bumper crop led to 10 million acres of cotton plowed under and 6 million piglets slaughtered. (Source: First New Deal)
15. Who the AAA left behind — Big farms got the largest payments; tenant farmers and sharecroppers were often evicted; the Southern Tenant Farmers Union organized in response. (Source: First New Deal)
16. The NIRA and the NRA — Industry codes of fair competition on prices and wages; more than 500 codes; the Blue Eagle. Image: Blue Eagle poster (alt text: an eagle gripping a gear and lightning bolts above the words 'We Do Our Part'). (Source: First New Deal)
17. The PWA — $3.3 billion for public works; more than 34,000 projects. (Source: First New Deal)
18. The TVA — Dams for flood control and electricity in the Tennessee Valley; about 15,000 families displaced. Image: Wilson Dam (alt text: a long concrete dam spanning a wide river). (Source: First New Deal)
Section: Critics and the Second New Deal
19. Critics on all sides — The American Liberty League attacked the New Deal as too radical; Townsend, Coughlin and Huey Long said it didn't go far enough. Image: WPA-era poster. (Source: Second New Deal)
20. The Supreme Court pushes back — In 1935 the Court struck down key parts of the First New Deal. (Source: Second New Deal)
21. The Second New Deal — Emergency Relief Appropriation Act of 1935: $4.8 billion, nearly a third for the new WPA. (Source: Second New Deal)
22. Court packing — Roosevelt's plan would have added six justices, expanding the Court from nine to fifteen. (Source: Second New Deal)
23. Social Security and the Wagner Act — A safety net for the elderly, unemployed, disabled and young; workers' right to organize protected. (Source: Second New Deal)
Section: Who was left out
24. African Americans and the New Deal — Overt discrimination in hiring for the CCC, CWA and WPA. (Source: Second New Deal)
25. Women and the New Deal — Women were excluded from many job programs; Frances Perkins in the Cabinet. (Source: First and Second New Deal)
Section: Wrap-up
26. Did it work? — It reversed the slide and built lasting institutions, but World War II brought lasting recovery. (Source: Second New Deal)
27. Exit ticket — Three questions: one program, one critic, one group left out.
