## Plan
1. Title — Clean hands, every time. Image: a nurse using a wall-mounted sanitizer dispenser (alt text: a nurse in scrubs pressing a hand sanitizer dispenser outside a patient room).
2. Why it matters — Some healthcare workers need to clean their hands up to 100 times a shift. (Source: CDC Hand Hygiene for Healthcare Workers)
3. The moments — Before touching a patient; before an aseptic task; after contact with blood, body fluids or contaminated surfaces; after touching a patient or their surroundings; moving from a soiled to a clean body site; right after removing gloves. (Source: CDC)
4. Sanitizer first — In most clinical situations use alcohol-based hand sanitizer: it kills germs more effectively than soap, is quicker between patients, and is gentler on skin. (Source: CDC)
5. What's in it — At least 60% alcohol. (Source: CDC)
6. How to rub — Cover all surfaces of hands and fingers, focus on thumbs, fingertips and between fingers, and rub until dry, about 20 seconds. (Source: CDC)
7. When soap and water instead — Visibly soiled hands, before eating, after the restroom, and caring for patients with suspected or confirmed infection during C. diff or norovirus outbreaks. (Source: CDC)
8. How to wash — Wet hands, apply soap, rub for at least 15 seconds, rinse, dry with a disposable towel, and use the towel to turn off the faucet; avoid hot water. (Source: CDC)
9. Gloves aren't a shortcut — Gloves don't replace hand hygiene; clean hands before putting gloves on and after taking them off, and change gloves between patients. Image: hands removing a glove (alt text: one gloved hand peeling a glove off the other inside out). (Source: CDC)
10. Nails and jewelry — Natural nails no longer than the fingertip; no artificial nails or extensions with high-risk patients such as ICU or OR patients. (Source: CDC)
11. Surgical hand antisepsis — Remove rings, watches and bracelets; scrub hands and forearms for the time the product's maker recommends, usually 2 to 6 minutes; ten-minute scrubs aren't needed. (Source: CDC)
12. The evidence and recap — In one 500-bed hospital, compliance rose from 38% to 63% after sanitizer was introduced. Recap: the moments, the two techniques, gloves. (Source: CDC, citing Boyce et al.)
