## Base plan
Story: Plates meet in only a few ways, and each way builds a different landscape. Four sections, then a wrap-up.

1. Title — Plate boundaries: where Earth's surface is made, destroyed and dragged sideways.
Section 1: Plates and boundaries
2. Earth's surface is broken into plates — The lithosphere is broken into plates that move slowly. (Source: USGS, This Dynamic Earth)
3. Most action happens at the edges — Four kinds: divergent, convergent, transform, and broad plate boundary zones. (Source: USGS, Understanding plate motions)
4. A map of the boundaries — World map with boundary types color-coded. (Source: USGS)
Section 2: Divergent boundaries
5. Spreading centers — Plates pull apart and magma makes new crust; the Mid-Atlantic Ridge spreads about 2.5 cm a year. (Source: USGS)
6. Iceland splits in two — Iceland sits on the ridge between the North American and Eurasian plates; cracks widen near Krafla. (Source: USGS)
7. Africa is tearing — The Red Sea and the East African Rift as young spreading zones. (Source: USGS)
Section 3: Convergent boundaries
8. Subduction and trenches — One plate dives under another; trenches are the deepest parts of the ocean floor. (Source: USGS)
9. Ocean meets continent — The Nazca Plate under South America lifts the Andes; also the Cascades. (Source: USGS)
10. Ocean meets ocean — The Pacific Plate under the Philippine Plate forms the Marianas Trench and island arcs. (Source: USGS)
11. Continent meets continent — India colliding with Eurasia pushed up the Himalayas and the Tibetan Plateau. (Source: USGS)
12. Convergent summary — Three kinds of collision, three kinds of landscape. (Source: USGS)
Section 4: Transform boundaries
13. Sliding past — Plates grind past each other; crust is neither made nor destroyed. (Source: USGS)
14. The San Andreas — About 1,300 km long; the Pacific Plate slides past the North American Plate at about 5 cm a year. (Source: USGS)
15. Why California shakes — Transform motion and earthquakes. (Source: USGS)
Wrap-up
16. Recap — Four boundary types, the landscapes they build, and the hazards they bring.
