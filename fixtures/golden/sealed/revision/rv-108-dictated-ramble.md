## Base plan
Story: Your hands are the main way germs travel from patient to patient. Clean them at the right moments, the right way, every time.

1. Hook — A hospital hallway at shift change: count how many surfaces one nurse touches between two rooms.
2. Why it matters — Some healthcare workers clean their hands up to 100 times a shift. (Source: CDC Hand Hygiene for Healthcare Workers)
3. When to clean your hands — Before touching a patient, before an aseptic task, after body fluids, after touching a patient or their surroundings, moving from a soiled to a clean body site, right after removing gloves. (Source: CDC)
4. Sanitizer is the default — Use alcohol-based hand sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol in most situations, unless hands are visibly soiled. (Source: CDC)
5. Hand rub technique — Cover all surfaces, focus on thumbs, fingertips and between fingers, rub until dry, about 20 seconds. (Source: CDC)
6. Handwashing technique — Wet, soap, rub at least 15 seconds, rinse, dry with a disposable towel, use the towel to turn off the faucet. (Source: CDC)
7. Gloves don't replace hand hygiene — Clean hands before putting gloves on and after taking them off; change gloves between patients. (Source: CDC)
8. Nails and jewelry — Natural nails no longer than the fingertip; no artificial nails with high-risk patients. Photo of short natural nails. (Source: CDC)
9. The evidence — In one hospital, compliance rose from 38% to 63% after sanitizer was introduced. (Source: CDC, citing Boyce et al.)
10. Practice break — Pairs practice hand rub with glow lotion and a UV light.
11. Common mistakes — Missing thumbs and fingertips, wiping off sanitizer before it dries, skipping hand hygiene after gloves.
12. Recap — The moments, the two techniques, and why gloves aren't a shortcut.
