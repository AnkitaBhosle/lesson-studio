## Base plan
Story: Small amounts plus time add up, because interest earns interest. Guideline in force: no individual financial advice.

1. Hook — "What if your $2 a day could turn into thousands?"
2. What compound interest is — Interest you earn on your interest, not just on the money you put in (the principal). (Source: Investor.gov)
3. The $100 example — $100 at 5% a year is $105 after one year and $110.25 after two; with nothing added it passes $162 in 10 years. (Source: Investor.gov)
4. The pizza example — A $2 slice every day is $730 a year. Save that once and invest it at 5%, and in five years it's about $931.69; in 30 years, about $3,155. (Source: Investor.gov)
5. The Rule of 72 — Divide 72 by the interest rate to estimate how many years it takes to double; at 9%, about 8 years. (Source: Investor.gov)
6. Why starting early matters — The longer the money sits, the more of the growth comes from interest on interest. (Source: Investor.gov)
7. Disclaimer — "This is general education, not financial advice."
