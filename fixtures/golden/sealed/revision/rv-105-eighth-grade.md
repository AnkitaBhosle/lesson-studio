## Base plan
Story: Every cell has a skin that decides what gets in and out. Here's what it's made of and why it bends instead of breaking.

1. Hook — "Every cell in your body has a skin. What is it made of, and how does it know what to let in?"
2. The plasma membrane — The plasma membrane delimits the cell and mediates its interaction with the extracellular environment via selective permeability and receptor-mediated signal transduction. (Source: Biology, 5.1)
3. Amphiphilic phospholipids — Phospholipids are amphiphilic: a polar, phosphate-containing head group and two nonpolar fatty-acid tails, which self-assemble into a lipid bilayer in aqueous solution. (Source: Biology, 5.1)
4. Integral and peripheral proteins — Integral proteins span the hydrophobic core, often as alpha-helical transmembrane domains; peripheral proteins associate with membrane surfaces. (Source: Biology, 5.1)
5. The glycocalyx — Glycoproteins and glycolipids on the extracellular face form the glycocalyx, which mediates cell recognition; HIV exploits CD4 glycoproteins. (Source: Biology, 5.1)
6. Membrane fluidity — Unsaturated fatty-acid kinks and cholesterol modulate fluidity across temperature ranges. (Source: Biology, 5.1)
7. Recap — The fluid mosaic model integrates lipids, proteins and carbohydrates into a dynamic, selectively permeable boundary.
