## Base plan
Story: The Bill of Rights in plain words: what the first ten amendments protect, one or two at a time.

1. Hook — "What rights do you have that the government can't take away?"
2. What it is — The first ten amendments to the Constitution, spelling out people's rights in relation to the government. (Source: National Archives)
3. The First Amendment — Freedom of speech and the press, the right to gather and protest, the right to ask the government to fix problems, and freedom of religion; the government can't set up or favor a religion. Narration walks through each freedom with a school example. (Source: National Archives)
4. The Second and Third Amendments — The right to keep and bear arms. No forcing people to house soldiers in their homes, which British laws had allowed before the Revolution. Narration tells the colonial backstory at length. (Source: National Archives)
5. The Fourth, Fifth and Sixth Amendments — No unreasonable searches or seizures. Grand juries for serious charges, no being tried twice for the same crime, no being forced to testify against yourself, due process. A speedy public trial, an impartial jury, knowing the charges, and a lawyer. Narration gives a separate story for each amendment. (Source: National Archives)
6. The Seventh and Eighth Amendments — Jury trials in federal civil cases. No excessive bail or fines and no cruel and unusual punishment. Narration repeats the jury idea from slide 5. (Source: National Archives)
7. The Ninth and Tenth Amendments, and recap — Rights not listed still exist; powers not given to the federal government belong to the states or the people. Recap of all ten. (Source: National Archives)
