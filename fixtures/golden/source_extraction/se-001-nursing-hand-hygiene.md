## Source
Week 2 notes, clinical skills.

Start with why: most infections picked up in hospital travel on hands. Students think they wash enough. They don't.

When: before you touch a patient, before anything clean or sterile (dressings, lines), after anything that might be body fluid, after you touch a patient, and after you touch the patient's surroundings even if you never touched them.

Which one: alcohol rub is the default. Soap and water when hands look dirty, and after caring for someone with diarrhoea, because the rub doesn't work well on some spores.

How long: rub until dry, roughly 20 to 30 seconds. Washing takes longer, 40 to 60 seconds including drying.

Bare below the elbows: no rings except a plain band, short nails, no artificial nails. They hide bacteria and you can't clean under them properly.

Demo: glow gel and UV lamp, then show them what they missed (usually thumbs and fingertips).
