## Source: notes
Big question to open with: how does an idea travel when every book is copied by hand?

Before: monks and paid scribes, months per book, very few copies, mostly in Latin, mostly owned by the Church and the rich.

Gutenberg, Mainz, around 1450: metal movable type, oil-based ink, a press adapted from wine presses. Bible printed in the 1450s. Not the first printing ever: block printing in China, movable type in China and Korea centuries earlier. What was new was the combination and how fast it spread in Europe.

Effects to cover (pick the strongest for 10th graders): Luther's ideas spread in weeks, not years. Cheaper books, more people learn to read. Languages get standardised spellings. Scientists can share exact diagrams.

Activity idea: give half the class one hand-copied page to copy, the other half a printed stack. Ask who finished first and whose copy has mistakes.
